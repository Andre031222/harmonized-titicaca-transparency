\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit for your consideration the manuscript \textit{``Harmonization and
validation limits of satellite Secchi retrieval in Lake Titicaca''} as a research article.

Lake Titicaca (8{,}372~km$^2$, 3{,}810~m a.s.l.) is the largest lake in South
America and, by volume, the largest tropical lake in the world. It is a
transboundary Ramsar site and the freshwater reserve for millions of Andean
people, yet its water quality is documented only by infrequent field campaigns.
It is precisely the kind of large lake, outside the well-instrumented systems,
that we understand the journal's scope to cover.

The manuscript is as much about \emph{how to evaluate} a machine-learning
water-quality product as about the product itself. We assemble 1{,}002 real
satellite--field match-ups (812 carrying a Secchi reading) against twelve years
of IMARPE limnological monitoring, and show that two defaults imported from the
turbid-water literature fail in clear, campaign-sampled water. For each we give
a diagnostic that any study can run.

\textbf{(1) Land-derived cross-sensor harmonization is invalid over dark
water.} The coefficients of Roy et al.\ (2016), fitted on vegetation and soil
and widely reused to merge Landsat into Sentinel-2 archives, carry additive
intercepts that exceed the entire native over-water Landsat signal by factors
of 1.4 to 69.8. A classifier still identifies the source sensor from the
\emph{already harmonized} reflectance with 99.8\% accuracy against a 66.3\%
chance rate: the retrieval was learning a sensor label, not unified optics. We
propose a classifier two-sample test as a one-line check for this failure.

\textbf{(2) The blocking unit for cross-validation must be diagnosed, not
assumed.} Blocking by monitoring station is the standard remedy for spatial
autocorrelation, but in a campaign-based programme it retains almost nothing:
under campaign-blocked folds a station-mean null model still reaches
$R^2=0.208$ and a zone-mean null $0.287$, while the campaign-mean null
collapses to $-0.012$. Station blocking leaves the reported accuracy
unchanged from a random split ($R^2=0.596$ in both cases), which is evidence
that it retains nothing rather than that no leakage exists. We give the
null-model procedure that identifies the correct unit.

\textbf{(3) An honest retrieval with calibrated uncertainty.} Under
campaign-blocked validation a Random Forest retrieves $Z_{SD}$ with
$R^2=0.574$ and RMSE 1.86~m ($n=812$), against 0.097 for a correctly bounded
classical blue/green band ratio. Split-conformal intervals are well calibrated
marginally (89.4\% empirical coverage at a 90\% nominal level) but not
conditionally, falling to 71.3\% in the clearest water --- a limitation a
single coverage figure would hide. Skill collapses under leave-one-zone-out
extrapolation, and we report explicitly that chlorophyll-$a$, suspended solids
and thermal temperature are \emph{not} retrievable in this oligotrophic
regime.

We are aware that our headline accuracy is modest by the standards of the
turbid-lake literature. That is the point: we show it is the honest number for
this class of water under a defensible design, and that the higher figures such
studies report are partly an artefact of the blocking unit and of baselines
implemented as straw men.

All data and code are openly available at
\texttt{https://github.com/Andre031222/harmonized-titicaca-transparency}. The
analysis is reproducible end to end from the deposited match-up tables without
Earth Engine credentials, and every number, table and figure in the manuscript
is generated by that pipeline rather than typed by hand.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare
no competing interests. We have no preferred or excluded reviewers.

Thank you for considering our work.
